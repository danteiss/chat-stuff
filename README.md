Tell me about astigmatism
Astigmatism is a very common, highly treatable refractive error—not an eye disease—that causes blurred, fuzzy, or distorted vision at all distances.
To understand how it works, it helps to look at how a healthy eye focuses light compared to an eye with astigmatism.
What Causes Astigmatism?
Normally, the clear front window of your eye (the cornea) and the internal lens are shaped smoothly and evenly, like a round basketball. This allows incoming light rays to bend (refract) equally and focus on a single sharp point on the back of your eye (the retina).
With astigmatism, the cornea or lens is curved unevenly, shaped more like an oval football or the back of a spoon:
Corneal Astigmatism: The surface of the cornea has asymmetrical curves.
Lenticular Astigmatism: The lens inside the eye is unevenly curved.
Because the curves are uneven, light rays get bent in different directions and focus on multiple spots either in front of or behind the retina. This creates a stretched, smeared, or double-shadowed image.
Key Symptoms
People with astigmatism commonly experience:
Blurry or Distorted Vision: Objects may look stretched, tilted, or shadowed at both near and far distances.
Eye Strain & Fatigue: Your eyes constantly work harder to try and force a sharp focus.
Headaches: Frequent headaches, especially after reading or working on screens for long periods.
Night Vision Struggles (Halos/Starbursts): Streetlights and headlights spread out, streak, or show large glare bursts when driving at night.
Squinting: Constantly squinting to try and bring blurriness into focus.
Types of Astigmatism
Astigmatism often occurs alongside other refractive errors:
Myopic Astigmatism: Combined with nearsightedness (trouble seeing far away).
Hyperopic Astigmatism: Combined with farsightedness (trouble seeing close up).
Mixed Astigmatism: One principal direction is nearsighted, while the other is farsighted.

Astigmatism does not heal on its own, but it can easily be corrected:

The simplest and safest option. Lenses are built with a cylindrical correction to compensate for the uneven curve of the eye.
